
In absence of potential: $V(x) = 0$

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{d^2 \psi}{dx^2} = E \psi \longrightarrow \text{Time-independent Schrödinger equation}$$

$$\implies \frac{d^2 \psi}{dx^2} + k^2 \psi = 0 \quad \text{where } k^2 = \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}$$

Solution to the Free Particle System:

$$\psi(x) = e^{ikx}, \quad k \in \mathbb{R} \longrightarrow \text{Momentum eigenstate (state with definite momentum)}$$

Can be viewed as sum of $\sin$ and $\cos$ terms and describes a complex-valued wave of wavelength $\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{\vert{}k\vert{}}$.

$$\implies -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} (ik)^2 e^{ikx} = E e^{ikx}$$

$$\implies E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m} \qquad \text{Classical interpretation: } E_{\text{classical}} = \frac{p^2}{2m}$$

Comparing both dispersion relations, we get:

$$p = \hbar k \quad \left(k \longrightarrow \text{wavenumber}\right)$$

$$\implies \vert{}p\vert{} = \frac{2\pi\hbar}{\lambda} = \frac{h}{\lambda} \longrightarrow \text{de Broglie wavelength of particle}$$


Wavefunction $\psi = e^{ikx}$ is not normalisable:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} dx \, \vert{}\psi\vert{}^2 = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} dx \, 1 = \infty$$
