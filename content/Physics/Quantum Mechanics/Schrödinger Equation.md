
$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi(x,t)}{\partial t}
=
\hat{H}\psi(x,t)$$
 $\hbar \approx 1.054571817 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s} \approx 6.582119569 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{eV\,s}$ carries same dimension as $\mathbf{L}=m\,\dot{\mathbf{x}}\times\mathbf{x}$ and $S=\int dt\left(\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^{\,2}-V\right)$.

Consider a non relativistic particle, moving in 3D in presence of potential energy V(x):

In Classical Mechanics : 
$$H(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{p})
=
\frac{\mathbf{p}^2}{2m}
+
V(\mathbf{x})$$
In Quantum Mechanics:

$$\hat H
=
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2
+
V(\mathbf{x})$$
Structural similarity:

$$\mathbf{p}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\pm i\hbar\,\nabla$$
Plugging it in Schrödinger equation:

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t}
=
\left(
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2
+
V(\mathbf{x})
\right)
\psi(\mathbf{x},t)$$
$$
\implies \frac{\partial \psi(x,t)}{\partial t}
=
-\frac{i}{\hbar}
\left(
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}
+ V(x,t)
\right)\psi(x,t)
$$
