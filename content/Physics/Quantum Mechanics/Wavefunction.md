State of a particle is determined by a complex valued function:
$$\psi(x,t)$$
If we have information of $\psi(x,t)$ at some time $t_0$ then we have information of the state at all the time.

State: Collection of normalisable complex functions with equivalence relation.

Properties Of Wavefunction:

- A wavefunction must be normalised to have any probabilistic interpretation.

- If wavefunction differs by a constant complex phase then its probability distribution remains unaffected but other observable quantities change.
$$\psi(x,t)=e^{i\alpha}\,\psi(x,t)\ ,\ for \ any \ constant\ \alpha$$
- Multiplying wavefunction with position independent phase doesn't change the state.

$$\psi(x,t)\equiv \lambda\,\psi(x,t) , \qquad \lambda \in \mathbb{C} \neq 0 $$
- $\psi(x)$ can be viewed as vector in an infinite dimensional vector space, whose components are labeled by continuous variable x, $x \in \mathbb{R}$
