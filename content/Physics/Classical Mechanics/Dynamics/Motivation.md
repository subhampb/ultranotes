### Kinematics

- **Kinematics describes mechanical motions without considering their causes or whether such motions can actually occur in nature.**

### Space & Geometry

- **The concept of space arose from our experience of bodies having length, position, and definite arrangements relative to one another.**
- **Geometry is a mathematical formulation of properties and relations associated with bodies.**
- **Lobachevski: “We only comprehend motion in space... All other concepts, for example, geometry, are artificially derived... from the properties of motion.”**
- **Space and matter are inseparably linked.**

### Geometry & Experiment

- **Geometrical concepts are abstractions of real relations between bodies; hence geometry is an experimental science.**
- **Different geometries can be logically consistent; experiment determines which geometry describes the real physical world.**
- **Euclidean geometry is only an experimentally supported model of physical space, not an absolute truth.**
- **General relativity states that geometry in a gravitational field is non-Euclidean.**

### Point Mass

- **A point mass is a physical object mathematically represented as a point having mass, with no internal geometrical structure, shape, or size.**
- **Whether an object can be treated as a point mass depends on the problem and the question being asked.**
- **Point masses are auxiliary mathematical entities, not representations of actual atoms or molecules.**

### Rigid Body

- **A rigid body is one whose different parts preserve their relative positions, so its shape remains unchanged.**
- **A perfectly rigid body is one for which the distance between any two of its points remains unchanged.**
- **Euclidean geometrical concepts are inseparably linked with the ideas of perfectly rigid bodies and their motion.**

### Reference Frame

- **A reference frame is the set of spatial points together with the body relative to which their positions are determined.**

### Coordinate Systems

- **A coordinate system is essentially an agreement for assigning unique “addresses” to points in a reference frame.**
- **Different coordinate systems can describe the same physical space; choosing the right one can determine the success of solving a problem.**
- **The position of a point in three-dimensional space is characterized by three numbers.**
- **The procedure of assigning numbers to spatial points is called arithmetization of space**


### Important lines — **Time**

- **Time reflects the fact that processes have duration, occur in a certain sequence, and develop through stages.**
- **Time cannot be separated from matter and its motion; speaking of time independently of processes is meaningless.**
- **Periodic processes provide the basis for comparing and measuring durations.**

### Time in reference frames

- **The rate of physical processes can differ at different points in a reference frame; therefore, time need not be the same everywhere.**
- **Near Earth's surface the difference is extremely small, so a unified time can be introduced to very high accuracy.**
- **Rotating or accelerating reference frames also prevent the introduction of a unique time.**
- **Reference frames are divided into inertial and noninertial frames; inertial frames are those in which Newton's first law applies.**

### Synchronization of clocks

- **A process occurring from one point to another cannot have its duration measured by a single clock; clocks at the two points must be synchronized.**
- **“Simultaneously” cannot be used to define synchronization, because simultaneity itself has to be defined physically.**
- **Synchronization therefore requires a physical procedure involving signals between clocks.**
- **Light provides the natural signal for synchronization because its propagation velocity is finite and universal.**
- **In an inertial reference frame, the velocity of light is independent of the motion of the source and receiver and has the same value in every direction.**
- **Two clocks separated by distance ss are synchronized when their readings differ by s/cs/c according to the light-signal procedure.**
- **An event occurring at a point at time tt means that the synchronized clock at that point would read tt when the event occurs.**
