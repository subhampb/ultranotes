

$$U = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2} \int d^3 x \, \mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{E}, \qquad \mathbf{E} = \frac{e}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}$$

$$= \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2} \cdot \frac{e^2}{16\pi^2 \varepsilon_0^2} \int_a^\infty \frac{4\pi r^2}{r^4} \, dr$$

$$= \frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{1}{a}$$


The energy in the electric field cannot be greater than the observed mass of the electron $m_e$:

$$m_e c^2 > \frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{1}{a} \implies a > \frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{1}{m_e c^2}$$



Size of electron $\longrightarrow$ **Compton wavelength**

Distance scale at which an electron gets surrounded by a swarm of electron-positron pairs:

$$a \sim \frac{\hbar}{m_e c}$$


Using the above equations, we get:

$$\frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar c} \ll 1$$

Similar to the **Fine-Structure Constant**:

$$\alpha = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar c} \approx \frac{1}{137}$$
